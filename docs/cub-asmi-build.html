Build process · Cub + ASMI

Build a Cub with the ASMI indenter

Turn a Genmitsu 3018-PROVer V2 into an automated indentation tester that measures the mechanical properties of samples in a 96-well plate. Work through the five parts in order; each ends with a short check so you know it worked before moving on.

The five parts

  1. AAssemble the gantry1.5–2 hoursBuild the Genmitsu 3018-PROVer V2 kit exactly as Genmitsu describes, except for the spindle, acrylic baffle and cable ties noted below. Then fit the ASMI force sensor.
  2. BBring up the gantry30–45 minutesSet the controller's firmware so the machine homes to the back-right-top corner and every axis moves the right way. CubOS relies on this and never flips axes in software.
  3. CInstall CubOS15 minutesInstall CubOS, the software that runs the gantry and the force sensor, and open its Operator window.
  4. DCalibrate the gantry15–20 minutesTeach CubOS where the deck is and how far each axis can travel, using a printed calibration block and the indenter tip.
  5. ECalibrate labware10 minutes per plateRecord exactly where your 96-well plate sits so the indenter lands in the centre of every well.

What you need

Hardware

  • Genmitsu 3018-PROVer V2 CNC router kit
  • Vernier Go Direct Force and Acceleration Sensor, with a 3 mm or 5 mm ball indenter
  • Printed parts: Cub 3018 mount, Cub Vernier Go Direct mount, Cub calibration block and base (STL files in the CAD viewer and Cubware)
  • 2× M5 × 15 mm button-head screws and 2× M5 hex nuts
  • A 96-well plate
  • A Mac or Windows computer

Tools

  • The Allen wrenches and Phillips wrench included with the Genmitsu kit
  • Calipers
  • A 3D printer, if you're printing the mounts yourself

Software

  • Universal Gcode Sender (part B only)
  • CubOS (part C)
Caution

Never leave the gantry running unattended during bring-up or calibration, and keep the E-stop within reach whenever it's powered.
