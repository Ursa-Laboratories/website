# Concentric ASMI Genmitsu V2

![Concentric ASMI assembly](ConcentricAsmiAssembly.png)

A new two-piece mount for the **Vernier Go Direct Force and Acceleration
sensor (GDX-FOR)** on the **Genmitsu V2 52 mm spindle holder**. The sensor
stands above the holder and its force post points down through the original
spindle centerline. Remove the spindle and the 43 mm holder/adapter; use the
52 mm holder supplied with the purchased kit.

**Prototype CAD, not physically validated.** The printed parts have passed
offline solid, mesh, and nominal fit checks. Actual sensor retention,
concentricity, clamp grip, connector access, full-machine clearance and
measurement compliance still require the checks below. The gray holder and
orange sensor in the illustrations are simplified reference envelopes.

## Dimensions and evidence

All dimensions are millimeters. Z=0 is the **top face of the metal spindle
holder**. Z increases upward; X=Y=0 is the spindle/indenter axis. The sensor's
wide face runs across X; its thickness runs along Y.

The original pocket is shallower than Vernier's published housing size, so this
design does **not** assume the old pocket is a dimensionally exact sensor model.
The centered post passage in the old CAD informs the alignment; measure the
actual force-post axis against the purchased housing before accepting this as
a concentric installation. `force_axis_x` and `force_axis_y` compensate a
measured post offset by shifting the housing pocket while keeping the spindle
passage at X=Y=0. Their defaults are zero.

## Indenter reach and Z clearance

The bracket uses the upgraded assembly's existing stroke; it does not create
additional Z travel. Moving the sensor above the holder requires sufficient
probe length to reach through it. With the nominal centered housing, its lower
face is at Z=8.3 and the holder underside is at Z=-36. The total projection
from the housing's lower face to the tip must therefore exceed **44.3 mm**
just to emerge below the holder.

The illustration uses a **6 mm diameter, 62 mm projection** dummy probe, giving
17.7 mm nominal exposed length below the holder. Those are visualization
parameters, not measurements of your indenter and not a fabricated probe
design. For 15 mm exposed length, the minimum projection is 59.3 mm with this
housing position. Increasing length affects stiffness, buckling and usable
working height. Check your existing indenter before printing the full mount.

The force accessory uses **#6-32 threading**, per Vernier; do not substitute
an M3 or M4 screw. Thread engagement and probe construction require the actual
accessory dimensions. The sensor's force range is not a load rating for this
printed mount or an extended indenter.

The reference holder is only a 76 × 76 block with its 52 bore and 36 height;
its square outside, carriage, guides, motor, screws, switches and cables are
not a manufacturer CAD assembly. In particular, the 90.6 mm tall sensor cage
must be checked against the fixed Z mechanism at the upper endpoint. No
full-machine collision-free stroke is claimed.

## Hardware and printing

- One body, one cover; the fit coupon is not installed in the final assembly.
- Four M4 × 45 socket-head screws and four standard M4 hex nuts. The cover has
  8 mm diameter × 4.5 deep head recesses; the rear has 7.3 across-flats × 3.5
  deep nut pockets. Verify the purchased hardware's head and nut dimensions.
  At nominal dimensions each screw projects about 3.9 mm behind the rear face;
  include that projection in the carriage clearance check.
- Thin, hard shims selected after measuring the actual housing. Nominally
  0.3 mm per face; use equal opposing shims to preserve the axis. Do not use
  compressible foam in the axial force path. No tightening torque is specified
  until the printed material and actual housing fit are validated.

For an initial fit prototype, use PETG or a material you have already qualified
for fixture use; start with 0.2 mm layers, 6 perimeters and 50% infill. These are
prototype print settings, not a strength qualification. Print the body upright
on its cylindrical end with a brim and supports under the projecting floor
and upper cradle roof. Keep support scars out of the seating and bore surfaces.
Print the cover with its broad outer face down and pocket upward; inspect
bridges around the access windows and head recesses. Print the coupon flat.
All STL coordinates use the assembled position; let the slicer place each part
on its bed. Do not print the reference envelopes.

## Assembly and operator validation

![Exploded cover](ConcentricAsmiExploded.png)

1. **Measure first.** Confirm GDX-FOR housing width, thickness, length, force
   axis relative to both opposing housing faces, post/indenter projection,
   actual 52 mm holder bore and its height. Check that the metal holder has a
   flat annular top seat for the 60 mm shoulder.
2. **Try the coupon.** With the spindle removed and machine power off, insert
   the ring in the 52 mm holder. It should enter by hand and grip securely when
   the clamp is tightened normally. An undersized or loose part needs a revised
   stem diameter; forcing an oversized print or tightening the clamp to its
   closed stop is not a fit solution. The short coupon does not qualify the
   full stem's grip or taper.
3. **Prepare the cradle.** Clear supports and burrs. Trial-fit the four nuts
   and screws without the sensor. Keep all hardware out of the sensor pocket.
   Set the body in the 52 mm holder with the shoulder seated. Orient the broad
   sensor face across the gantry, and check rear nut/screw access and clearance.
4. **Fit the sensor.** Place it in the rear cradle with force post downward.
   Fit equal opposing hard shims as measured, including the axial seats. Attach
   the cover evenly; retain its designed split gap. The sensor must not rock
   or translate under reversal of a small manual load, and its case must not
   visibly distort. If either occurs, revise the fit rather than tightening
   harder. The cover fasteners assemble the cage; they do not compensate the
   nominal housing clearance automatically.
5. **Check the force path.** Fit the actual indenter and any required correctly
   threaded accessory. Ensure only the sensor's force post carries the probe;
   the probe must remain clear of the entire 20 mm tunnel. Verify coaxiality
   against the spindle datum in both horizontal directions. Nominal CAD offset
   is zero; actual clearance and shimming introduce positioning uncertainty.
6. **Check the machine envelope.** With power off and the Z axis supported,
   the operator can use the kit's manual knob to inspect both Z endpoints.
   Check sensor roof, rear screws, shoulder, USB cable and indenter against
   the carriage, motor, guides, limits, sample holder and deck. Do not force a
   hard stop. Record the usable stroke and lowest/highest tip positions with
   the intended sample holder. The published 56 mm stroke is not a substitute.
7. **Check retention and compliance.** The shoulder supports downward loading;
   upward indentation reaction is resisted by the metal clamp's grip on the
   printed stem. It has no positive anti-pullout fastener. Before experiments,
   use a supported bench fixture and controlled low loads in both directions
   to check slip, rotation, backlash and sensor zero shift. Progress only within
   a separately chosen test load; record deflection against an independent
   reference and define acceptance limits appropriate to the experiment.
   Polymer creep and long-probe bending can affect indentation results.
8. **Re-establish the instrument datum.** After physical acceptance, the
   operator must re-zero the force sensor in its installed orientation and
   re-establish XY/tool-height and surface references in CubOS. Previous offsets
   from the front-mounted ASMI are not valid for this geometry. This design
   task has not changed or exercised any CubOS machine configuration.

## Offline verification

The builder checks one valid closed solid per printable part, reloads every
exported STEP to confirm validity and volume, reloads every printable STL to
check closed-solid status, one connected component and consistent orientation,
and checks nominal intersections among body, cover, housing, holder and probe.
The body and cover have zero modeled overlap with each other or the reference
hardware. Both the probe and holder bore use X=Y=0. These checks validate the
modeled geometry, not physical alignment, stiffness or an unmodeled carriage.

macOS installation used here the executable is
`/Applications/FreeCAD.app/Contents/Resources/bin/python`, with the app's own
`/Applications/FreeCAD.app/Contents/Resources/lib` in its module search path.
